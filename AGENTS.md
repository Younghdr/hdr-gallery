# Project design preferences

## Language

- All Chinese copy, labels, metadata, and user-facing responses must use Traditional Chinese. Never use Simplified Chinese.

## Layouts to avoid

- Do not use the recurring composition with a large empty left column, an oversized headline confined to the right, and two small text columns underneath.
- Do not create empty space only to make a section look editorial. Every major area should contain an image, useful information, navigation, or deliberate visual texture.
- Give different chapters distinct but familiar editorial structures. Do not repeat one template everywhere, but do not use experimental or gimmicky layouts merely to be different.
- Do not make Chinese display type so large that it dominates a 4K viewport or resembles a presentation slide.
- On 4K desktop layouts, do not leave a large empty left region and do not force Chinese headings into arbitrary line breaks. Keep complete phrases readable in their natural sentence rhythm.

## Preferred direction

- Favor photography-led travel editorial layouts with strong full-width images and readable Chinese narrative.
- Use English sparingly for labels, coordinates, route names, and metadata.
- Keep Chinese headings compact enough to read as a complete phrase at once.
- Vary section composition according to its content using established travel-editorial patterns: full-width photography, caption rails, route timelines, field-note blocks, maps, or compact single-column essays.
- On 4K displays, improve line length, spacing, image scale, and information density instead of simply scaling typography up.
- For three-part editorial headers with a label, Chinese headline, and supporting paragraph, reserve enough width for the headline to keep each complete phrase on one line. Do not allow a short final fragment or punctuation to wrap onto a separate line.
